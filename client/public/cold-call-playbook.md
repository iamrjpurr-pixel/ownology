# Ownology · Cold-Call Playbook

**Purpose**: bullet-proof scripts for the founding-partner outreach phase.
**Landing page**: `ownology.ai/join?ref=firstname-lastname`
**Attribution**: check `/admin/contacts` filtered by `source=cold-call`.

---

## Guiding principles (before you dial)

1. **You are not selling. You are inviting.** Founding-partner framing means they're doing you a favour by joining, not the other way around.
2. **Respect their time.** They may be mid-ferment. Never assume they have more than 60 seconds unless they give it to you.
3. **The URL is the pitch.** Your job on the call is to get them to open `ownology.ai/join` — the page does the rest. Every path leads there.
4. **Qualify quickly.** Boutique, AU/NZ/US, currently making calls on live fermentations. If none of those, wish them well and hang up warmly.
5. **Book only the ones who ask.** Don't push a 20-min call on someone who isn't leaning in. Their time matters more than your pipeline.

## The 30-second opener (word-for-word)

> *"Hi Sam — Rich Purr from Ownology, based in Adelaide Hills.*
> *I'm building a cellar intelligence platform for boutique winemakers and I'm onboarding twelve founding partners this quarter. Given you're mid-ferment with the 2026 vintage, I thought I'd give you a call.*
> *Do you have 60 seconds for me to explain what it does, or would you prefer I just send you the link and you can look at it when you're not covered in must?"*

**Why this works**:
- Names them + names you first (permission tone, not spam).
- "Twelve founding partners" = scarcity without pressure.
- "Mid-ferment 2026" = you understand their world.
- The double-option close (**explain OR send link**) — either path is a win, so they can't refuse the whole call.

## Branch A: "Send me the link"

> *"Perfect. It's ownology dot ai slash join. I've personalised the page for you — your name's on the top-line greeting when you land.*
> *Have a look when you have two minutes. If you want a proper 20-minute chat, you can book straight from that page. If not, no chase, no follow-up. Cheers Sam — enjoy the ferment."*

**End the call in under 90 seconds.**

**What happens next**: They land on `/join`, see the flash cards + form, and either book or don't. Their click is logged in `/admin/contacts` with `source=cold-call`, `ref:sam-firstname-lastname`, `tags: cold-call, founding-partner`. Watch for a form submit within 48 hours — that's a lead worth chasing.

## Branch B: "Yeah, explain"

Now you've been given a real minute. Use it. **Three sentences, then stop and ask them what they think.**

> *"Sure — three things.*
> ***First***, *every morning at 7am, an AI cellar-hand called Owen writes you a Cellar Brief: what happened in your tanks yesterday, what needs action today, ranked by risk, all cited from the Red and White Wine Bibles. You read it with your coffee.*
> ***Second***, *any question you'd normally text your consultant at 3am — Ownology answers it, from the same reference library, and cites the source. Every answer becomes a permanent journal entry the whole industry can search — with your name on the ones you asked.*
> ***Third***, *founding partners get lifetime pricing, a monthly call with me, and feature requests jump the queue. You basically get to shape what a cellar-intelligence platform should look like."*
>
> *"What's the biggest pain in your cellar right now?"*

**The final question is the qualifier.** Their answer tells you if you should keep talking or wrap up.

- **Real pain named** (stuck ferments, staff turnover, compliance panic) → move to Branch C (Book the call).
- **"Nothing really, all good"** → *"Then honestly, you don't need me right now. But have a look at the page when you get a chance — ownology dot ai slash join. If it clicks, book a chat. If not, no chase."* End call warmly.
- **"Not sure"** → same as above. Don't force it.

## Branch C: Book the 20-minute chat

Once they've named a real pain, close on the calendar. Not on the phone.

> *"That's exactly the sort of thing Ownology is built for — I'd love to show you what it looks like for your setup.*
> *Best way: go to ownology dot ai slash join, book the 20-minute chat, and I'll come prepared to your specific problem. Or I can email you the link right now — what works?"*

**Don't try to demo on this call.** Cold-call demos = bad demos. Get them to the landing page + book a proper call = good demos.

## Objection handlers (top 5)

### 1. *"We already use Vintrace / VinX / Grape Cru Control."*

> *"Great — Vintrace nails compliance and inventory. Ownology's not competing with that. What we do is layer intelligence on top: the daily brief that reads your logs, the AI that answers 'what would you do?', the journal that captures your reasoning for next vintage. Most of our founding partners use both. Vintrace for the records, Ownology for the thinking."*

### 2. *"I've got a spreadsheet system and it works fine."*

> *"Honestly? Best winemakers I know all say that. Nothing wrong with spreadsheets. The question is what happens when your assistant winemaker leaves, or when you can't remember why you added DAP on day 4 last vintage. Ownology's not about replacing your spreadsheet — it's about making sure the reasoning behind every entry is captured and searchable. Have a look at the sample journal entries on the page — that's the difference."*

### 3. *"I've got a consultant I already trust."*

> *"That's gold, keep them. Ownology's the thing you use at 3am on a Saturday when you can't call the consultant. Or when you want a second opinion cited from the actual references. It's not consultant-replacement — it's consultant-multiplier. If the consultant's advice aligns with what Owen says, you know you're on solid ground."*

### 4. *"I don't trust AI to make winemaking calls."*

> *"Same. You shouldn't. Nothing in Ownology makes calls for you — it presents the data, cites the reference, and you decide. Owen writes 'here's what the Red Wine Bible says about this scenario', not 'do this'. Winemaker still drives. Have a look at any sample answer on the page — every line has a citation."*

### 5. *"I'm too busy — vintage is on."*

> *"That's exactly the point of building it now. Founding partners who come in during vintage help us pressure-test the tool with real fermentation data — you literally shape what it becomes. And you don't need to give me time now — book the 20-min chat for post-ferment, in July. The page is ownology dot ai slash join. Cheers Sam."*

**End warmly. Never argue.** If they don't want it, they don't want it. Move on to the next call.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do**:
- ✅ Introduce yourself with **full name + city** (permission tone).
- ✅ Give them an exit at every branch.
- ✅ Say the URL slowly, twice, when they agree to look.
- ✅ End the call in ≤ 3 minutes.
- ✅ Log every call outcome in `/admin/contacts` — even the "not interested" ones (adds them to the "don't chase" list).
- ✅ Send the follow-up email within 30 minutes if they didn't answer.

**Don't**:
- ❌ Never pitch the price on the call. Let the page do that.
- ❌ Never try to demo the product on the phone.
- ❌ Never argue with an objection — restate their concern back, add one honest response, then let them go.
- ❌ Never call the same winemaker twice in one week without a genuine new reason.
- ❌ Never oversell "AI" — you'll lose the winemakers who're already skeptical of it.

## Metric to watch (in `/admin/contacts`)

- **Landing page click-through**: source=cold-call rows appearing (means they at least visited the URL).
- **Form submissions**: rows with all four tags `cold-call · founding-partner · ref:name · note:something` (means they engaged).
- **Booking close rate**: submissions ÷ calls made.
- **Target for first two weeks**: 50 calls → 20 landings → 5 form submits → 2 booked calls → 1 founding partner. If those ratios hold, you need 300 calls to get all 12 partners.

---

## What to do after each call (30 seconds)

1. Open `/admin/contacts`.
2. Find or create the row for the winemaker.
3. Add a note: **outcome (not-interested / voicemail / interested / booked)**, one-line reason, any next-step.
4. Move on to the next call.

**Do not let the CRM lag behind the calling.** Founder time is the most expensive resource — feed the pipeline while it's warm.
